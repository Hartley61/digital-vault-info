Digital Vault is a living record of the information that matters to you. It can remain private or be shared securely with people you explicitly invite, allowing trusted participants to view and update the same current vault. It also supports continuity, recovery and eventual handover, but it is designed for active use during your lifetime rather than only as a legacy archive.

Sharing is private and invitation-only. It does not make the vault public or give access to anyone who has not accepted the owner's invitation.

Your control and privacy

Privacy cover and locking

After you authenticate, Digital Vault starts with your records covered. On iPhone and iPad, the blue unlock screen uses a shield symbol before this privacy cover appears. Select Open Vault when you are ready to view them. This is a quick visual privacy screen for shared spaces; it does not replace device authentication. Selecting Lock Vault ends the session and returns to the password, passcode or biometric unlock screen.

Digital Vault is designed as a user-controlled tool. You decide what to enter, whether to create a backup or archive, who to invite, and when to remove, restore or delete your information. The app does not choose your records, recipients or retention period for you.

If you share a vault, you are responsible for deciding whether the information is appropriate to share and for choosing trusted people. A private invitation does not make the vault public. Removing a participant stops future access to the shared vault, but cannot delete information they may already have viewed, copied or exported.

Digital Vault uses the Apple Account and iCloud services configured on your device for storage and synchronisation. Keep your device, Apple Account and backup locations secure, and review Apple's own privacy information for those services.

How Digital Vault protects your information

Digital Vault is designed so that you control the information in your vault. The app does not sell vault content, use it for advertising, or make decisions about you from it. Storage and synchronisation use Apple's CloudKit and iCloud services under the Apple Account configured on your device. The app also keeps working copies of vault data and attachments on each device where you use it. Apple documents that CloudKit protects data in transit and at rest; Apple, rather than Digital Vault, operates those services and controls the associated Apple Account security.

Digital Vault adds these application controls:

  • Device authentication and privacy cover. Unlocking uses the device's available biometric authentication or passcode. When the app leaves the foreground it locks, and its privacy cover is intended to prevent records being displayed before you deliberately open the vault.
  • Private, invitation-only sharing. A vault owner chooses each participant. Public sharing is disabled. Removing a participant stops their future access, but cannot recall anything they have already viewed, copied or exported.
  • Encrypted recovery backups. A .dvbackup is encrypted with the recovery password you choose. Digital Vault does not retain that password, so it cannot recover an encrypted backup if you lose it.
  • Local-only Digital Access secret. Where you add a Digital Access master password/access code, it is kept in the current device's Keychain and is not included in CloudKit synchronisation or recovery backups.

These controls reduce risk; they are not a guarantee against every threat. Digital Vault is not described as end-to-end encrypted: iCloud/CloudKit and Apple Account security are Apple services, with their own terms and security model. Use a strong device passcode, protect your Apple Account with multi-factor authentication, install operating-system updates, and share a vault or backup only with people and locations you trust.

Browse your vault

Digital Vault opens in All, which shows active Life Records. The active category is highlighted. Select a category to filter records and select a record to view its details. Mac normally keeps the category list visible. iPhone uses compact navigation. On iPad, the category list may be collapsed in portrait or remain collapsed after rotation; tap the sidebar button to show it as the permanent left-hand column in landscape.

The total displayed above All or the selected category is the number of active Life Records currently in that view. Archived records are excluded. The count is a quick orientation aid; it is not a substitute for searching when you need to find a particular record.

Medical records

Choose Medical for a provider or practice, condition, medicine, allergy, vaccination, procedure, test result or personal medical summary. Each record can say who it concerns, identify the provider and contact numbers, and include a medical reference. Medicine records can capture dose instructions, frequency and dates; allergy records can capture the recorded reaction and severity. Use Notes for other history and link prescriptions, results and letters through DocFiles. Dates are for reference; this release does not send medicine reminder notifications.

Organise DocFiles

DocFiles are grouped as Documents, Photos and Instructions. Use the Change Category control beside an editable file to move it between these three groups. This reclassifies the existing file: it keeps the same file, preview, folder and all of its linked Life Records. Moving a category is different from changing its folder, and neither action creates another copy of the file.

Archive and retrieve records

Open a record and choose Archive, then confirm. It leaves the active categories but retains its category, card information and linked documents.

Choose System → Archive to select it. The detail shows an Archived badge; linked files can still be opened. Choose Restore to Vault to return it to its original category. The app returns to All with the first active record selected. Archive is separate from DocFiles. Archiving a record does not delete its files.

For an archived record only, Delete Permanently removes the record after a separate destructive confirmation. Its linked documents stay in DocFiles, and links to other Life Records remain. Files without a folder appear in Unfiled. The record deletion cannot be undone. Use Restore to Vault for an ordinary archived record.

Bank cards

In a Bank record's Cards panel, choose Add Card. Enter the debit/credit type, name, number, expiry in MM/YY format and optional CVV2. Multiple cards may be stored for joint account holders. Show reveals one card; Hide conceals it. Revealed cards offer Edit Card and Remove Card. Leaving the record or backgrounding the app hides details again.

Cards share the vault's existing participant permissions. Encrypted backups include card information and attachments. Keep card details private.

Use DocFiles

Open DocFiles to manage general files, photos and instructions. On iPhone, tap Done to return to All.

On Mac and iPad, DocFiles uses the middle pane for folders and the right pane for the files in the selected folder. New files begin in Unfiled. Use the folder button beside a file to move it to an existing folder, create a new folder, return it to Unfiled, or cancel without making a change. The three-dot button beside a folder lets you rename it or delete it. Before folder deletion, the app reports how many files it contains; confirming returns those files to Unfiled and does not delete them. Folder moves and renames do not alter the file contents, preview, backup, or Life Record link.

Add each document or photo to DocFiles once. To associate it with a Life Record, open the record and choose Link Document or Link Photo, then select a DocFiles entry not already linked to that record. One file can link to multiple Life Records independently. Removing one link preserves the file and its other record links; permanently delete files from DocFiles.

Create a backup

  1. Open Backup & Restore.
  2. Enter and confirm a recovery password, then choose Create Encrypted Backup.
  3. Wait for the progress message and read the completion result.
  4. Tap OK to return to All with the first record selected.

Each backup is one password-protected .dvbackup file containing the Life Records and VaultFiles documents/photos needed to recover the shared vault. Digital Vault does not store the recovery password. Use one strong password for your backups and retain two secure copies outside Digital Vault. They may appear in iCloud Drive; use the Available Backups list inside Digital Vault to view the owner’s recovery copies.

The repository synchronises through the vault owner's iCloud account, so the same backups are available to the owner's authorised Mac, iPad and iPhone. Shared participants receive the current live vault through private CloudKit sharing, but do not manage or restore the owner's recovery backups.

Is my vault up to date, or do I need to restore?

A backup is a dated recovery snapshot. It is not a live copy of the vault and restoring it is not the way to check whether another device is current.

To check that the vault is up to date, compare one or two recent records or edits on the owner's devices. For a fuller check, make one harmless edit on one device, allow time for iCloud to synchronise, then confirm that exact edit appears on the other device. If the recent records and edits match in both directions, the vault is current and no restore is needed.

Restore only when records or files are genuinely missing or damaged and you have identified a particular backup as the authoritative recovery copy. A restore replaces the current local Life Records and VaultFiles; it can replace newer data. Before restoring, create a fresh backup of the current state if there is any possibility that you may need it later.

Restore a backup

  1. Open Backup & Restore.
  2. Select a dated entry under Available Backups, or choose Restore Latest iCloud Backup to select the newest entry automatically.
  3. For an encrypted .dvbackup, enter the recovery password. A wrong password is rejected before any vault data is changed.
  4. To restore a legacy portable backup stored elsewhere, choose Choose Other Backup, then select DigitalVault_Backup.json or its DigitalVault_Backup_… folder.
  5. Review the replacement warning and confirm the restore.
  6. Wait for the completion result, then tap OK.

Restoring replaces current Life Records and VaultFiles. Create a fresh backup first if you may need the current state later.

Use more than one device

On the owner's devices, use the same authorised Apple account and allow time for iCloud to synchronise. A backup created on the owner's Mac, iPad or iPhone can then be selected from Digital Vault on another owner device. Backups chosen through Choose Other Backup remain portable when their complete DigitalVault_Backup_… folder is kept together.

Changes normally appear quickly, but CloudKit delivery and import timing is controlled by the operating system. If a recent change made on an iPhone or iPad is not yet visible on the Mac, do not edit the older Mac value. Switch away from Digital Vault, return to it, unlock if required and allow a moment for the latest data to appear. The record list and open detail should then show the same imported value.

Install and update the beta

  1. Install Apple's TestFlight app on the test device.
  2. Open the Digital Vault invitation from the email or Messages link.
  3. Tap View in TestFlight, then Accept and Install.
  4. Keep the existing Digital Vault app installed when updating to a newer build.
  5. Confirm the version under Digital Vault → About. The current release candidate is version 3.0 Build 20. Build 20 has completed TestFlight acceptance on Mac, iPad and iPhone; access depends on your tester invitation.

If the invitation opens Digital Vault directly, sign in with the Apple Account named in the collaboration invitation and wait for the initial CloudKit import. In dedicated shared-vault mode, cached records stay covered until that first import succeeds, so the previous local dataset is not shown while current records and archives arrive. Do not delete the app or restore a backup during this import.

Owner administration and participant access

Only the vault owner manages participants. Open the sharing button and choose Manage Participants to view access status. Use Refresh People with Access to update the status. An accepted participant is shown as Accepted — can edit.

On the owner's Mac, enter the trusted person's exact Apple Account email and choose Grant Access and Copy Private Link. Digital Vault grants access only to that named Apple Account and copies a private invitation link. Paste that link into an email or message to the intended person. Only enter an address for someone you intend to invite.

On iPhone and iPad, use Invite Another Person and the system sharing sheet. A pending invitation means the recipient has not completed acceptance; it does not mean that current vault data has been synchronised. Use Remove only when you intend to revoke that person's access to the live shared vault.

Participants can edit shared records and linked DocFiles, but they cannot manage the owner's recovery backups, CloudKit schema or participant list.

Share your vault with a trusted person

  1. On the owner's device, tap the person-with-plus sharing button.
  2. On a Mac, open Manage Participants, enter the person's exact Apple Account email, choose Grant Access and Copy Private Link, and paste the copied link into an email or message. On iPhone or iPad, use the system sharing sheet to invite the person.
  3. The invited person opens and accepts the invitation while signed into that same Apple Account on a device containing Digital Vault.
  4. Allow time for the Life Records and attached documents to synchronize.

Invited participants have read/write access: they can add and edit records and attachments. Sharing is private to the people the owner explicitly invites; the vault is not publicly accessible. The owner can return to the sharing screen to review participants or stop sharing.

Security

Digital Vault locks when it leaves the foreground. Unlock it with the available biometric authentication or device passcode.

Private invitations and device authentication reduce access risk, but you should share only with people you trust. Backups can contain your records and linked files; keep them private and do not send a backup folder to someone unless you intend them to have its contents.

Identification records

Use Identification for passports, driving licences, birth certificates, marriage certificates and NHS details. The fields change with the selected document type. Link scans through DocFiles and record the physical location of original documents.

Finance types

Bank records include a Finance type: current account, savings account, credit card, loan, mortgage or other debt. Existing Bank records default to Current account until edited and saved.

Insurance and Utilities

Insurance fields change by policy type, including home, pet, life, critical illness and vehicle policies. Premium frequency can be monthly or annual. Utilities records identify the provider, account, service address, contact details, payment method, tariff, contract, balance and document location.

Digital Access

Use Digital Access for executor-facing instructions about password managers, email, cloud storage, domains and social-media legacy settings. Record the service, account holder, where access is managed, the action required and any closure or legacy instructions. An optional master-password or access-code field is hidden by default; use it only where appropriate and never store bank card numbers, CVVs or unrelated credentials.

Build 20 interface

The welcome screen introduces Digital Vault as a personal life record for you now and trusted family members or executors in the future. Select Unlock Vault to authenticate; the optional privacy cover still requires Open Vault. A new device may take longer to initialise and import iCloud data.

Record totals appear above All and individual categories, not beside sidebar labels. On iPad, the toolbar magnifying glass opens a search popover. Use Clear Search to remove a filter, including when there are no matches; changing category also clears it. iPhone and Mac retain native search fields.

DocFiles highlights the selected folder and displays its name above the file list. Use the eye button for Preview. The ellipsis menu contains Change Category, Move to Folder, Rename File and, when permitted, Delete File. Existing permission checks and deletion confirmation still apply.

On Mac, launch begins in a compact window. Authentication restores the saved working window size; later automatic locks preserve that working size.
